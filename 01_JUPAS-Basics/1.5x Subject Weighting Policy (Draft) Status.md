# 1.5x Subject Weighting Policy (Draft) Status

> [!tip] Short answer
> Proposed policy: English, Mathematics, ICT, M1, M2, Physics, Chemistry and Biology counted at 1.5x level points. Caveat: the published 2026 JS1204 formula is still best-5 at weight 1, so the 1.5x policy may or may not be on the admission site yet - confirm the year's official formula.

> [!info] 🎓 **Admission talk Q5** — the NEW 1.5x weighting policy · [⬅ back to cover page](../index.html#q5)

## Question

What is the new 1.5x weighting policy for CS and is it already on the admission site?

## Answer

CityUHK is emphasizing a **new weighting policy** under which eight discipline-related subjects are weighted at **1.5x** in the JUPAS admission score: **English, Mathematics, ICT, M1, M2, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology**. Under this policy each of these subjects contributes 1.5 times its level points; other elective subjects stay at weight 1.

**Status / important caveat:** The officially published 2026 JUPAS main admission score formula for JS1204 (BSc Computer Science) on the CityUHK admission site currently reads **best 5 subjects, weight 1** (no 1.5x applied), and the same for JS1218 and JS1221. The 1.5x policy is the approach the CS admissions team is promoting to applicants and **may or may not be reflected in the current admission site** at the time of reading. Always confirm the exact formula in the year's 'Admission Score Formula and Admissions Scores' PDF before applying.

Why it matters: 1.5x strongly rewards strong results in exactly the subjects a CS student will use. A student with 5s in English, Math and ICT gains far more under 1.5x than a student with 5s in unrelated electives - so subject choice in DSE matters for CS ranking.

**Worked comparison (same 5 subjects, two ways of scoring):**

| Scenario | Subjects (level) | Weight-1 total (published 2026) | 1.5x total (policy) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strong science profile | Eng 5, Math 5, ICT 5, Physics 4, Chem 4 | 5.5+5.5+5.5+4+4 = **24.5** | 8.25+8.25+8.25+6+6 = **36.75** |
| Mixed electives | Eng 5, Math 4, ICT 4, Econ 4, Hist 4 | 5.5+4+4+4+4 = **21.5** | 8.25+6+6+4+4 = **28.25** |

The gap between the two scenarios widens dramatically under 1.5x (12.5 points vs 3 points), which is the whole point of the policy: it differentiates applicants who actually studied the CS-relevant subjects.

## Examples

- If you are advising a student on DSE subject choice for CS, the 1.5x policy says: a 5 in Mathematics is worth 1.5x as much as a 5 in a non-listed elective, so prioritizing Math + ICT + one of Physics/Chem over a non-science elective improves the CS admission score under the policy.
